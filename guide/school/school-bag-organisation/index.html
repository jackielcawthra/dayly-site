School bag organisation

A bag with a system takes two minutes to pack. A bag without one takes fifteen and loses a jumper.

The Dayly Editors Published 12 August 2026 Updated 14 August 2026 2 min read Ages 5–16
The short answer

Give every item a fixed place in the bag, keep a short two-part list (every day, and depends on the day) attached to the bag, pack the night before, and build a four-step unpack habit at the door on arrival home.

Fixed places

A bag where everything has a place can be packed and checked at a glance. A bag that is one big compartment cannot.

  • Front pocket: the things that come out most — bottle, snack, keys, pass
  • Main compartment: books and folders, standing up, spines the same way
  • Side pocket: water bottle, always, so a leak does not reach the books
  • A small zip pocket: the things that get lost — money, notes, headphones, plasters
  • A named place for letters, in or out. This is the one nobody has and everybody needs.

The list, attached to the bag

Two parts, and it lives on the bag or where the bag lives — not on a fridge in another room.

Every day: bottle, pencil case, reading folder, homework diary, lunch or card. Depends on the day: PE kit, swimming things, library book, instrument, forms, money.

Pack the night before

Always. Why it matters — in short, the evening has slack in it and the morning does not.

The unpack habit

The half nobody teaches, and where most losses and missed letters happen. Four steps at the door, same order, every day:

  1. Bag off, on the hook
  2. Bottle out, into the dishwasher
  3. Lunch box out, emptied
  4. Letters out, into the tray

Ten seconds if it is a habit. If it is not, you get a fermenting lunch box on Thursday and a letter about a trip that closed on Tuesday.

Try this

Say the four steps out loud with them, in the same order, for a week. Do not do the steps — say them. They will start saying it themselves, which is the point.

Practicalities

Weight. A heavy bag on one shoulder is genuinely bad for a growing back. Both straps, tightened so it sits high, and take out what does not need to be there.

Water bottles. A leak destroys books and homework. One good bottle in a side pocket beats three cheap ones in the main compartment.

Kit bags. A separate bag for PE means it can be prepared independently and lives by the door on kit days. It also means the school bag stops being a laundry container.

Termly clear-out. Every bag accumulates: broken pencils, ancient letters, three-quarters of a sandwich. Once a term, empty it entirely.

What to try this week
  1. Empty the bag completely and give each thing a fixed place.
  2. Attach the two-part list to the bag itself.
  3. Add a named place for letters, in both directions.
  4. Run the four-step unpack at the door for a week.
