About

Why Dayly exists

It started as one household’s attempt to stop losing Thursdays.

Dayly began the way most useful things do: somebody got fed up. One parent was holding a household of six children between two homes, four school timetables, eleven activities a week, a linen rota, a cooking rota and a mental list of who had outgrown which shoes. None of it was written down anywhere that anybody else could read.

The first version was a single file that ran in a browser. It was built to answer four questions a family actually asks:

  • What needs doing now?
  • What am I responsible for?
  • Is everything done?
  • When did I last do it?

Everything since has come from that household and others like it, which is why Dayly has features that look strange from the outside — half-day presence, plain-English recurrence, a leave-by time that moves — and lacks features that look obvious. Those come from real weeks going wrong.

What we believe

A family is not a project to be managed. It is a set of people who live together, several of whom are learning how to run a life. Software that treats children as a workforce misses the point.

Responsibility should move in one direction. From parent to child, gradually, on purpose. Anything that keeps a parent permanently in the role of manager has made the problem worse while appearing to solve it.

Making the load visible is most of the work. You cannot divide what nobody can see. That is true between two adults and it is true between adults and children.

The system should not need a system. Anything requiring daily maintenance by one person has recreated the thing it replaced.

What we won’t do

We will not sell data, advertise to children, build a leaderboard between siblings, add streaks designed to be broken, or introduce location tracking. Those are not roadmap decisions we might revisit; they are the reason the product is shaped the way it is.

Where we are

Dayly is made in Sweden, hosted in the EU, and built by a small team. We answer our own email: hello@dayly.app.
