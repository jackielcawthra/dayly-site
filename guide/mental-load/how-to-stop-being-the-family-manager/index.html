How to stop being the family manager

Doing less does not help if you are still the only route to the answer.

The Dayly Editors Published 6 May 2026 Updated 5 August 2026 4 min read
The short answer

You stop being the family manager by making the information reachable without you and transferring whole areas including the noticing. Doing fewer tasks while remaining the only person who knows things simply converts you from a worker into a bottleneck.

There is a version of “doing less” that makes everything worse.

You stop doing the tasks. But you are still the only person who knows what the tasks are.

Now the household is waiting on your attention instead of your hands. That is a bottleneck, not a delegation.

Getting out of it takes three moves.

1. Get what you know out of your head

Anything that lives only in your head is a job you cannot put down.

The test is a week. Could you be uncontactable for seven days without briefing anybody?

Here is what would have to be written down for that to be true.

  • The week: who is where, when, and what they need with them
  • The recurring: bin day, kit day, club times, the Monday linen change
  • The standing: which child owns which job, which adult owns which area
  • The contacts: school, clubs, dentist, the neighbour with the spare key
  • The expiring: passports, subscriptions, the MOT

That is not a big document. Two sides of paper. One screen.

And it is the whole difference between a manager and a system.

2. Answer with where it lives, not with the answer

The habit that keeps you central is being helpful in the moment.

Somebody asks what time football is. You know. You say.

Four seconds. Every time. Forever.

Say this instead: ”It’s on the calendar.”

Do it for a fortnight. It is irritating, it feels withholding, and then it stops — because people learn where the information is and give up routing through you.

And if nobody can find it, that is worth knowing too. It means the system is not usable yet.

Try this

For one week, answer every household question with where the answer lives rather than what it is.

Keep a tally of how many questions you get each day.

Most people watch it halve inside five days.

3. Give away the noticing, not the doing

The last move is the hard one.

A task you hand over comes back to you when it is finished. An area you hand over does not come back at all, because somebody else is now the person who realises it is due.

Say it out loud: ”You don’t need to remember any of it.”

Then do not remember it.

Not visibly, anyway. The internal twitch takes a couple of months to fade. That is normal, and it is not a sign the arrangement is failing.

The children’s half of this

Children keep you central in a way that is easy to miss.

Every time you remember something on their behalf, you confirm that they do not have to.

A child who has been reminded about their PE kit every week for three years has had three years of practice at being reminded.

The alternative is not indifference. It is moving the check earlier — to the night before, when there is still time to fix it — and letting the occasional small consequence land.

What it looks like when it has worked

Somebody asks the calendar instead of asking you.

An area runs for a month and you did not think about it once.

A child packs a bag you never checked.

And you go away for a week, and the week simply happens.

What to try this week
  1. Write the two sides of paper: the week, the recurring, the standing, the contacts, the expiring.
  2. For one week, answer questions with where the answer is rather than what it is.
  3. Hand over one area with the sentence “you don’t need to remember any of it”.
  4. Move one check with a child from the morning to the night before.

Common questions

Is this not just making everyone else’s life harder?

Briefly, yes — and that is the trade. A household organised around one person's memory is fragile and exhausting for that person. The fortnight of friction buys a system that keeps working when they are ill, working late, or simply want an evening off.
