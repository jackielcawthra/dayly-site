Dayly vs a general task app

Task managers assume a self-directed adult with a laptop. That describes about half of one person in most households.

Written by the people who make Dayly. We have an obvious interest, so we have tried to say clearly where the alternative wins and who should not use ours.

What general task apps do well

  • Flexible projects with arbitrary structure
  • Fast capture, often the best in class
  • Powerful filtering for people who enjoy that
  • Shared lists — a shopping list in a task app is genuinely fine
  • Integration with work tools

If both adults in a household already use one and like it, a shared project is a reasonable way to run a family, and it is free or nearly free.

The four assumptions that break

That the user opens the app voluntarily. Not true of most children, and not true of the adult who is already overloaded.

That tasks are mostly one-off. Family work is overwhelmingly recurring, and recurrence in most task apps is expressed as an interval — “every 7 days” — which nobody in a household can act on.

That everyone is available. A task assigned to a child who is at their other parent’s house is noise, and noise is what makes people stop reading a list.

That assignment is delegation. In a task app, one person enters work and assigns it. That is management, and it is the thing that was supposed to be reduced.

What replaces them

Ownership rather than assignment, including the noticing. Plain-English recurrence — “every Monday”, “every other Saturday”, “when your basket is full”. Presence, half-day accurate. Different views by person, not one list filtered. A dated record rather than a completion count.

Be honest about the trade

A task app is more flexible than Dayly and always will be. If you want to model an arbitrary project with dependencies, use a task app.

Dayly is opinionated about what a family week looks like, which is exactly why it needs less configuration and exactly why it will occasionally not do the thing you want. That is the trade, and it is worth knowing before you start.

Common questions

Can I import from my current task app?

Not automatically. Most families find setting up from scratch takes about an hour and produces a better result, because a task list rarely translates into ownership and recurrence cleanly.

Other comparisons

Dayly vs a shared calendar

If your only problem is double-booking, stop reading and set up a shared calendar. It is free and it works.

Dayly vs a chore chart on the fridge

A chart on a wall has one enormous advantage over any app: it is always visible and needs no decision to open.

Dayly vs the family WhatsApp group

Almost every family runs partly on WhatsApp. It is a superb messenger and a terrible system of record.

Dayly vs a paper family planner

There is a reason the wall planner keeps selling. It is visible without anyone deciding to look at it.
