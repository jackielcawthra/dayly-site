How Dayly works

Stop being the only one who knows what’s happening.

1

Forward it

Send Dayly anything — an email, newsletter, PDF, photo or pasted text. You can even set up a rule so school and club emails go straight to Dayly.

2

Dayly reads it

Dayly finds the dates, times, places, kit and deadlines, and works out who it belongs to. You approve it before it goes into the calendar.

3

Everyone sees what’s theirs

One family calendar, with a colour for each person. Everyone sees their own plans, responsibilities and what’s coming up — without having to ask you.

The distinction the product is built on

A chore is a task you are told to do. A responsibility includes noticing it needs doing.

Both are useful. Only the second one ends with you not having to be there — and almost every family app on the market is built for the first.

What a child holds in Dayly

Permanent things. Their bag, their kit, the area of the house that is theirs — and until when, if it rotates.

Their days. The rota, with today marked. Whose turn it is to cook, whose washing day it is.

What is outstanding. Not framed as failure. Just what has not happened yet.

Tomorrow. What is on, what to pack, and what needs an adult to know something — checked the night before, when there is still time to fix it.

It grows with them

By age
4–6Pictures, two or three steps, the same order every day.
7–10Their own bag and kit, packed the night before against their own list. One area of the house.
11–13Their whole week: deadlines, kit, plans, their own washing day.
14–17Their diary, appointments, revision plan and packing. You stop being the reminder service.

You add the next thing when the last one has gone quiet — three weeks without you mentioning it.

What it deliberately does not do

No location tracking. No leaderboards between siblings. No streaks. No advertising. No dashboard of a child’s failures.

A child’s view belongs to the child. That is the whole point of giving them one, and an app that reads as surveillance will not be used — which would be the correct response.

Common questions

How do I know what my child is ready for?

Use the age-by-age generator, then check whether the previous responsibility has actually become automatic before adding anything.

Can I see whether they have done it?

You can see what is outstanding, because that is what a shared household list is. There is no monitoring view beyond that, deliberately.
