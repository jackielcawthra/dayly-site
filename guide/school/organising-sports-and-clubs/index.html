Organising sports and after-school clubs

Clubs generate more small, urgent admin per hour than anything else in family life.

The Dayly Editors Published 14 August 2026 3 min read
The short answer

Give each activity a permanent kit bag that lives by the door, put the whole season’s fixtures in the calendar when they are published, agree lift arrangements a week ahead rather than the day before, and hand each child responsibility for their own kit from about nine.

Clubs are the most admin-dense part of family life: a fixed time, in a different place, requiring specific equipment, with a fee, a group chat, and a bag that comes home wet.

One bag per activity, permanently

Not a bag that gets packed for each session. A bag that contains that activity, permanently, and lives by the door.

Football: boots, shin pads, socks, kit, water bottle. It never gets unpacked except for washing, and the washing goes back in immediately.

This single change removes most of the pre-session searching, and it makes the activity transferable to the child — because “get your football bag” is a thing a nine-year-old can do and “find your shin pads” is not.

The whole season at once

When fixtures, terms and fees are published, put all of it in the calendar in one sitting. Every match, every fee date, every break, every venue change.

It takes fifteen minutes and prevents an entire category of Saturday morning.

Lifts, agreed early

The most stressful part is rarely the activity. It is the twenty minutes when two children need to be in two places.

  • Sort lifts a week ahead, not the day before
  • Set up one regular swap with one other family. Regular beats ad hoc, because nobody has to ask
  • Put who is driving in the calendar, not in a conversation
  • Say no to the thing that does not fit, and say it at sign-up rather than in November

The kit cycle

The most reliably forgotten household routine:

Home → bag emptied → wet things out → wash → back in the bag. Not “at some point before the next session”.

Give it a fixed day. For twice-weekly activities, it is the evening after each session. This is a job a ten-year-old can own entirely, and it is one of the best first responsibilities available.

What the children own

By age
6–8Carrying their own bag. Putting wet things in the basket.
9–11Their kit, checked before leaving. Emptying the bag on return. Knowing their session times.
12–14The whole cycle, including washing. Telling the coach when they cannot come.
15+Their own sign-ups, fees discussed with an adult, transport where possible, and the entire kit cycle.

The honest question, once a season

Ask them, at a natural break rather than after a bad session: do you still want to do this?

Children continue with activities long after they have stopped wanting them, usually because they think an adult wants them to, or because stopping feels like failing. A season boundary is a free opportunity to find out, and the week that comes back is worth a great deal.

What to try this week
  1. Build one permanent bag per activity and put it by the door.
  2. Put the whole season’s fixtures and fees in the calendar in one sitting.
  3. Set up one regular lift swap with one family.
  4. Give the kit cycle to the child whose kit it is.
