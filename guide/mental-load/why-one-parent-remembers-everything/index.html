Mental Load

Why one parent ends up remembering everything

Nobody decided this. It happens through four small, sensible steps, and each one is reversible.

The Dayly Editors Published 18 March 2026 Updated 30 July 2026 4 min read
The short answer

One parent becomes the household index through drift, not decision: they are present when the systems are set up, they become the default contact, answering questions is faster than teaching, and the pattern hardens into "the one who knows" and "the one who helps". Reversing it means changing the contact details, not the effort level.

Ask a couple how it happened and neither of them can point to a moment.

There was no conversation. Nobody volunteered. It is almost never a decision.

Here is how it happens, in four steps. Each one has a way out of it.

1. Somebody is there first

Parental leave. A flexible job. A year where one person’s work happened to be quieter.

Whoever is present when a system gets set up ends up inside it. They register with the nursery. They meet the other parents. They learn which door the reception class comes out of.

The way out: re-do the registrations. Dull, and it works. Which is step two.

2. They become the point of contact

The school has one number. The dentist has one email. The class group has one member.

Every one of those was a form somebody filled in once. And every one of them now routes an entire category of information to a single person.

The way out: change the details. Put the other parent’s email on the school system, the clubs, the GP.

This one act moves more than a month of good intentions. Information arriving in your inbox is information you cannot help noticing.

Try this

Tonight, list every organisation that holds a contact number for your family. School, clubs, dentist, GP, optician, childminder, swimming, the class group.

Change half of them.

Do not split each one down the middle — that just doubles the noise. Give whole organisations to whole people.

3. Answering is faster than teaching

Your partner asks which bag the swimming kit is in.

You know. Telling them takes four seconds. Explaining the system takes ten minutes, and they will probably ask again next week anyway.

So you tell them.

Multiply that by a thousand small exchanges over a couple of years, and the knowledge is permanently in one place. Load-bearing.

The way out: answer with where the information lives, not with the information. “It is on the list.”

That is irritating for about a fortnight. Then it is over.

4. It hardens into who you are

Now there is a person who knows and a person who helps.

Both roles feel unfair from the inside. The one who knows feels like a switchboard. The one who helps feels like staff — asked to do things without context, corrected on the details, and told they are not doing enough.

The way out: stop dividing tasks and divide areas instead.

An area comes with the noticing. And noticing is the thing that was never actually shared.

Why “she’s just better at it” is a trap

It is usually said kindly. It works like a lock.

Nobody is born knowing when a child’s shoes have stopped fitting. You find out by being the person who checks.

So being good at it is a consequence of practice, and practice is a consequence of who got assigned. Treat the result as a cause and the assignment never comes up again.

But the same logic runs the other way, and that is the useful part.

Whoever takes an area on will, within a couple of months, be the one who is “just better at it”.

What to try this week
  1. List every organisation that holds a contact number for your family.
  2. Move half of them — whole organisations, not split ones — to the other parent.
  3. For one week, answer household questions with where the answer lives rather than the answer.
  4. Name out loud the areas each of you owns, so “helping” stops being the word in use.

Common questions

We both work full time and it is still uneven. Why?

Because the imbalance is usually in the contact details and the defaults, not in available hours. Two full-time parents can have wildly different mental loads if one of them is on every list. Check who receives the information before concluding it is about time.

Is changing the school contact really that significant?

It is the single highest-leverage change most families make. School generates a large share of family logistics, and it flows to whoever is on the form. Information you receive directly is information you own; information relayed to you is a task somebody else is managing.
