Beach holiday packing list for families

Two swimming costumes are worth the space. One will inevitably be wet when someone wants to put it on again.

The Dayly Editors Published 16 July 2026 Updated 22 August 2026 4 min read Ages 0–17
The short answer

Two swimming costumes per person, more sun protection than you expect, and fewer clothes than you think — most destinations sell the rest more cheaply than the luggage costs. Keep one beach bag permanently packed, with a list inside it, and let an older child own it.

Beach holidays are one of the easier family trips to pack for. You need less clothing than you think, but there are a few things that are worth having enough of — particularly swimwear, sun protection and anything that is difficult to replace once you are on the beach.

The simplest approach is to pack light, keep a beach bag ready to go, and let children take responsibility for some of it.

The things worth getting right

  • Two swimming costumes per person
  • High-factor sun cream
  • Sun hats
  • UV swim tops for younger children
  • After-sun
  • Suitable footwear for sand, rocks or the pool
  • A small first-aid kit
  • Reusable water bottles

Two swimming costumes are worth the space. One will inevitably be wet when someone wants to put it on again.

Sun protection deserves similar attention. Hats and UV clothing can make life easier with younger children, particularly when reapplying sun cream to a wet, sandy child is becoming a daily negotiation.

Clothes for a week

You probably need less than you think.

  • 5–6 tops
  • 3–4 pairs of shorts or skirts
  • 1 long-sleeved layer
  • 1 pair of long trousers
  • 1 slightly smarter outfit
  • Underwear and socks — one per day plus two
  • Light pyjamas
  • 2 swimming costumes
  • Sun hat

Most beach destinations have shops and laundry facilities, so there is little point filling a suitcase with clothes that may never be worn.

The beach bag

A permanently packed beach bag makes mornings much easier.

  • Beach towels — quick-dry ones save space
  • Sun cream
  • Water bottles
  • Snacks
  • Goggles
  • Something to play with
  • Books
  • A bag for wet things
  • Small first-aid supplies
  • Some cash, protected from sand and water

This is also a good responsibility for a child. An older child can pack the bag, carry it and check that the important things are still there before you leave the beach.

You can keep a simple list inside the bag so that checking it becomes part of the routine rather than something a parent has to remember.

What to buy there

Some things are simply not worth carrying across a country or onto a plane.

Buckets and spades, inflatables, cheap beach toys, basic toiletries and sometimes even beach towels can be bought at the destination. It is worth checking prices before you leave, but there is little benefit in using valuable luggage space for inexpensive things that are readily available.

Documents and health

Keep the important things separate from the beach equipment:

  • Passports and any required entry documents
  • Travel insurance
  • Check that insurance covers planned water sports
  • EHIC/GHIC or equivalent healthcare card
  • Prescriptions in their original packaging
  • Copies of important documents stored separately

Check passport and entry requirements well before departure rather than assuming everything is valid because the passport itself has not expired.

Before you leave

Try on last year’s swimwear, sun hats and sandals before packing them. Children can outgrow summer clothes surprisingly quickly, and discovering that a swimsuit no longer fits when you arrive is not particularly useful.

Then give each child a small part of the packing that genuinely belongs to them.

A younger child can pack their own beach clothes. An older child can take responsibility for their beach bag and personal belongings.

The aim is not to make children responsible for the whole holiday.

It is to let them practise looking after something useful, in a situation where forgetting a book or a pair of goggles is much easier to learn from than forgetting something important at home.

A good beach holiday does not require a perfectly packed suitcase.

It requires the few things that matter, a little preparation, and gradually less of the responsibility sitting in one person’s head.

What to try this week
  1. Buy the second swimming costume for everyone.
  2. Build the beach bag now and give it to a child to own.
  3. Check swimwear from last year still fits before you go.
