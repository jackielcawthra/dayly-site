What a trip contains

A family holiday is a project with a hard deadline and a single point of failure who is usually also the person driving to the airport.

Dayly holds four parts of it:

Documents. Passports and their expiry dates — the one packing mistake that cannot be fixed at the destination — plus tickets, insurance, health cards and the accommodation address.

Packing, per person. The adults get the shared list: documents, medicines, chargers, first aid, sun cream. Each child gets their own, in their own words, which they tick off themselves.

Preparation. What has to happen before you go: the kit that needs checking eight weeks out, the prescriptions, the neighbour with the key, the heating.

Getting there. A leave-by time worked back from the actual check-in, including travel, parking and the walk. Every argument on the morning of departure is really about a leave time nobody agreed.

Why the per-person list matters

Children from about seven can pack their own bag against a written list, and from about eleven without a check. That is one of the easiest responsibilities to hand over — the list is finite, the deadline is known, and the consequence of forgetting is instructive without being serious.

The rule that makes it work: what is not packed does not come. It does not work if an adult carries a shadow bag of the things they forgot.

Reusable

A trip in Dayly can be copied. The second ski holiday takes ten minutes to set up, because last year’s lists are already there — including the things you swore you would remember next time.

Common questions

Can children tick their own list?

That is the point of it. Each child sees their own list on their own view and ticks it themselves.

Does it remind me about passports?

Expiry dates go in as dates, so they surface well before the trip rather than the week before. It is the single highest-value thing in the travel section.
