How the Dayly Guide is written

What we will and will not claim, who writes it, and how to tell us we are wrong.

Who writes it

The Dayly Guide is written and edited in-house by the team that builds Dayly — parents, and a small editorial team. We publish under The Dayly Editors rather than inventing individual bylines with credentials attached.

We are not clinicians, teachers, psychologists or researchers. We do not have a medical reviewer, and we will not put “reviewed by” on an article to borrow authority we have not earned. If a subject needs professional input, we say so and point you to someone who has it.

What we claim, and how

We try to keep four things clearly separated, in every article:

What the research shows. Linked to the original source wherever possible, not to somebody’s summary of it. Where the evidence is correlational, we say so. Where a study is small, contested or old, we say that too.

What researchers are uncertain about. Named explicitly. Most useful questions in parenting do not have a settled answer, and pretending otherwise is the most common failure in this genre.

Practical suggestions. Things that work in households. Often not research-backed, and labelled as practice rather than evidence.

Opinion. Ours, marked as ours.

What we will not do

  • Say “research proves” when research shows a correlation
  • Cite a single study as though it settles a question
  • Use neuroscience decoratively. Brain-imaging language does not make advice more true
  • Invent statistics, or repeat a striking figure without finding its source
  • Use fear or guilt. There is a version of parenting content that works by making the reader
  • anxious. We are not writing that
  • Imply that a product solves a developmental problem. Dayly is a place to keep a system. It is not
  • an intervention

Sources

Evidence-heavy articles carry a visible Sources & further reading section with links to the original work. We prefer peer-reviewed research, systematic reviews, government and public-body guidance, and reputable educational organisations, in roughly that order.

Where we link to a paywalled paper, we say what it found so the article stands without it.

Updates and corrections

Articles carry a publication date and an updated date, and both are real. When we change something material, we update the date. When we get something wrong, we correct it and say what changed.

Tell us at hello@dayly.app. Corrections from readers who know a field better than we do are the most useful email we get.

Commercial content

Some articles contain affiliate links, always disclosed at the top of the article and covered by our affiliate policy. Nothing is included because it pays, our own product is always named as ours, and we say plainly where it is the wrong choice.

Why we publish any of this

Partly because it brings people to Dayly, and it would be strange to pretend otherwise. Mostly because the advice available on these subjects is unusually poor — long on confidence, short on sources, and frequently designed to make the reader feel bad. We think we can do better than that, and this page is the standard we would like to be held to.
