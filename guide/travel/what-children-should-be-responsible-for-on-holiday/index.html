What children should be responsible for on holiday

A week away is the best practice a child gets at looking after their own things.

The Dayly Editors Published 6 August 2026 Updated 14 August 2026 3 min read Ages 4–17
The short answer

Children can own their own bag, their journey bag, their sun cream and their daily kit from primary age, and by their teens can navigate, budget, plan a day and pack unchecked. Holidays are low-stakes practice — the consequence of forgetting a towel is instructive rather than serious.

A holiday is an unusually good week for handing over responsibility. The stakes are lower than at home — a forgotten towel is annoying rather than serious — and there is time to let a child be slow.

It is also the week when parents most often do everything, because it is meant to be relaxing and doing it yourself is faster. Which is a shame, because it is the best practice available.

By age

By age
4–6Their own small backpack. Carrying it. Putting their shoes somewhere findable. Bringing their towel back from the pool.
7–9Their own bag packed with help. Their journey bag entirely. Their own sun cream — applying the parts they can reach. Their things back to the room each day.
10–12Packing their whole bag against a list. Owning the beach bag or the day bag. Their own money for the week. Choosing one thing the family does.
13–15Packing unchecked. Navigating with a map or a phone. Ordering for themselves. Planning one day of the trip. Managing a budget across the week.
16–17Their documents, their money, their plans. Going out without an adult, where it is reasonable. Being genuinely useful — driving a leg, cooking, translating, sorting a problem.

The daily version

The habits that make a holiday work are the same three every day:

Their things come back with them. From the beach, the pool, the restaurant. A quick look round before leaving, by them.

Their bag is repacked. Each evening, for whatever tomorrow is. Ninety seconds.

Their kit is dealt with. Wet things out, sandy things out, nothing left in a bag overnight to be discovered on Thursday.

Try this

Give one child the job of the daily look-round before you leave anywhere. It is a real, useful responsibility and it is the reason families lose fewer towels.

Money

A holiday is an unusually good place for a child’s first real budget: a fixed amount, a known period, visible trade-offs, and no way to top it up.

Give it at the start, in cash, and let them spend it badly on day two. The lesson lands harder in a week than it would in six months at home, and it costs whatever you were going to spend anyway.

Planning a day

From about eleven, let each child plan one day: where, how you get there, what it costs, what time you need to leave. Then do it, including the parts that turn out badly.

This is the single most transferable holiday responsibility, and it is the one children remember — partly because it is genuinely difficult, and partly because being taken seriously is memorable.

What stays yours

Documents, medication, insurance, money that matters, and anything where the failure would be serious rather than instructive. Handing those over is not independence, it is just risk.

What to try this week
  1. Give each child the age band above and say it out loud before you travel.
  2. Hand one child the daily look-round.
  3. Give a fixed budget in cash at the start and do not top it up.
  4. Let one child plan one day, and go through with it.
