How Dayly works

Stop being the only one who knows what’s happening.

1

Forward it

People and presence

Who is in the household, and when — the layer everything else depends on.

Everyone in one household
Adults and children, each with a name, a colour and a set of initials that stay readable when two names collide.
A view per person
A child opens Dayly as themselves and sees their own things. They cannot switch to a sibling.
Half-day presence
Someone can be here for dinner and not for breakfast. Presence is recorded by half-day, not by day.
Fortnight patterns
Alternating weeks, handled by ISO week parity — the pattern most shared-custody arrangements actually run on.
Exceptions
One Tuesday a month, a club night away, a different week in the holidays.
Nothing falls due when you are away
A job never appears on a day its owner is elsewhere, and the cooking rota skips whoever is out.
Simplified child view
For children too young to read a full list: fewer steps, larger targets, pictures where words would not help.

The day

Not a list — a planned day, laid out around the things that cannot move.

A day that is laid out, not listed
Jobs carry a duration and either a time or a window, arranged around waking, the leave time, training, dinner and bedtime.
It says when the day will not fit
Rather than presenting eleven things and four hours as though they were compatible.
Before-leave scheduling
Jobs that must happen before you go are scheduled backwards from the actual leave time, which moves daily.
Retiming by drag or tap
Move a job to another time. Adults can remove one outright.
Tomorrow, tonight
A preview of the next day while there is still time to do something about it.
Day-off-only jobs
Big jobs wait for a weekend instead of surfacing on a Tuesday morning.

Responsibilities and jobs

Ownership rather than assignment — including the noticing.

Named owners
A job belongs to a person, not to a rotating column. Habits form; nobody checks whose turn it is.
Areas of the house
A room has someone who resets it, with a written standard and a history of when it was last done.
Rotation you choose
Weekly, fortnightly, monthly, every two months, termly — or never. Calendar rotations turn over on the 1st.
Plain-English recurrence
"Every Monday", "every other Saturday", "when your basket is full". Never "every 7 days".
Standing jobs
Some things happen when they need to, not on a schedule. Each person gets their own row and their own last-done.
Optional jobs
Nice if they happen, never counted as overdue, never worth nagging about.
Seasonal jobs
Things that only apply for part of the year read as out of season rather than overdue.
A dated record
Every tick stores a date and a name and is never overwritten. "When was the bathroom last done" has an answer.
Swapping and offers
Offer a job to someone else. A child taking an adult’s job earns points; child to child is an IOU.

School

The largest single source of family logistics, and the one that arrives as paper.

Term dates, holidays and closed days
Entered once, for the year. Assumptions are flagged as assumptions rather than shown as fact.
Timetables per child
Start and finish times that differ by day and by child, which is how they usually are.
Exception days
A day that differs — a late start, a different pick-up, a trip — overrides the timetable and says so.
Kit days
What each child needs, on which day, on their own list rather than in a parent’s memory.
Homework and projects
A long project becomes dated steps that arrive slowly, instead of all at once on the 27th.
Deadlines with an act-by date
The date it is due, and the date you have to start, buy or sign in order to make it.
Contacts per child
Teacher, mentor, office. A child sees their own; the rest stay adult-facing.
Adults-only school information
Absence reporting, leave applications, the staff list. Invisible under a child’s profile.

The school run and getting there

A leave-by time, worked backwards
From the earliest start, including travel and the walk from the car.
It names who is left waiting
If one child starts an hour before another, that is surfaced as a warning rather than buried.
Journey times per destination
Held per address, so a club on the other side of town is not assumed to be twenty minutes.
Not live traffic
There is no traffic API and the page says so. The arrow opens a maps app for the real thing.

Calendar

Four calendars, one week.

One view, a colour per person
School, clubs, appointments, matches and who is where.
A child’s own events, in their words
"Football, 17:30, Dad is driving" — not "E. training 17:30 Ekhagen".
Recurring activities
A weekly timetable, kept separate from one-off events.
Term-dated events
The four dated sessions of a book club, rather than a weekly rule that is wrong most weeks.
Calendar export
A standard .ics feed, so family events can appear in the calendar you already use.

Food and shopping

Meals for the week
Planned against who is actually home, and who is cooking.
A cook rota that respects training
Each child cooks once a week, only on evenings they are here and free — and it reports who it could not place, and why.
Recipes
Yours, pasted or imported. Household dietary patterns are enforced, not just noted.
A shopping list built from the meals
Plus anything anyone adds by typing it.
Tick boxes sized for one hand
Because the list is used in a shop, by a child, holding a basket.
Batch cooking and the freezer
Portions counted in and out, with anything old flagged to eat next.

Home

Beds and towels, per piece
Not per room — which sheets and which pillowcases, changed when, by whom.
Laundry days per person
A named day each, plus "or any time your basket is full".
Maintenance
Filters, alarms, the boiler, the car, the things that are annual and forgettable.
A Fix It list
The running list of what is broken, which everyone can add to.
A house handbook
Wifi, the stopcock, the alarm, bin day — what a house-sitter always asks. Entries can be marked adults-only.
Contacts
Plumber, electrician, doctor, school, insurance, landlord.

Travel

A packing list per person
Adults get the documents and the shared things; each child gets their own to tick.
Documents and expiry dates
Passports surface well before the trip rather than the week before.
A leave-by time for the trip
Worked back from check-in, including parking and the walk.
Reusable trips
Copy last year’s ski holiday, including the things you swore you would remember.

Money and motivation

Deliberately narrow. It works perfectly well switched off.

Points for extra effort
Taking on someone else’s job, or doing something nobody asked for. Not for being a member of your own household.
Adult awards
A book, an hour of training, helping for fifteen minutes — each worth what your household decides.
Paid and owed
Points marked paid, and IOUs between children tracked honestly.
Pocket money
Amounts, days and conditions, held alongside the rest rather than in someone’s head.

How it behaves

The settings that decide whether it feels like help or like being managed.

No notifications by default
A place you look, not a thing that shouts. Reminders exist and are off unless you ask.
Nothing punishes a gap
No streaks, no resets, no leaderboard between siblings. A missed fortnight costs nothing.
Choose what a child sees
Only their own, their own plus the shared household things, or everything.
Choose what a child can do
Tick only, add their own, or full editing.
Missed jobs behave how you choose
Stay overdue, roll on, or disappear.
Codes rather than passwords
Short enough for a six-year-old. A curtain between siblings, and we say plainly that it is not more than that.
Works offline
Writes queue and sync when you are back. Shops have bad signal.
Works on a shared screen
A spare tablet in the kitchen, as well as phones.
Export everything
The whole household leaves as a file whenever you want it.

What is not built

Some of it is on the way. Some of it never will be, and it is worth saying which.

Live two-way calendar sync
Export is a standard .ics feed. Full sync with Google or Apple needs a server and OAuth, and we would rather ship it properly than half-ship it.
Location tracking
Not a gap. It will not be built.
Messaging outside the household
No friend requests, no usernames, no way for a stranger to exist.
A monitoring view for parents
A parent sees what is outstanding. There is no dashboard of a child’s failures, deliberately.
Dedicated kitchen-display hardware
The week view works on a spare tablet, but we do not make a wall panel. If that is the main thing you want, buy from someone who does.
