What a family organiser has to do that a calendar cannot

A calendar holds events. A family week is mostly not events.

It is knowing that Thursday is a kit day, that the reading folder goes back on Wednesday, that the bins go out on Friday, that Emma’s boots have stopped fitting, and that somebody has to reply to the trip letter by the 14th. None of that has a start time, so none of it belongs in a calendar — and it ends up in one person’s head instead.

Dayly is built for that second category: the recurring, the owned, the noticed. Alongside the calendar, not instead of it.

What is in it

  • The week, with a colour per person and only the things that need to be known
  • Household jobs with a named owner and a day with a name — “every Monday”, not “every 7 days”
  • Areas of the house, each reset by a person, rotating on whatever schedule you choose
  • School: term dates, kit days, deadlines, forms, and the things that come up twice a term
  • Routines: morning, evening, homework, Sunday
  • Trips: a packing list per person, and a leave-by time worked back from the departure
  • Meals and shopping, built from each other
  • A view for every child, in their own words, with their own things

The part that makes it different

Every job has an owner, and every child has a screen showing what is theirs. That is not a presentation choice — it is the whole design. A household organiser where a parent assigns work each day has digitised the mental load rather than reduced it.

Who it suits

Households with more than one child, where school, clubs and household jobs all need to live in the same place, and where one person is currently carrying most of the noticing.

It is more than you need if what you want is a shared calendar between two adults. We say that plainly on our comparison page.

Common questions

Does it replace our shared calendar?

It can, and it does not have to — Dayly exports to a normal calendar so events can appear alongside your work diary. What it adds is everything that is not an event: the jobs, the owners, the kit days, the recurring things.

Can the children use it?

That is what it is for. Each child opens it with their own code and sees their own view. From about seven most children use it independently.
