Family travel documents checklist

The only packing mistake that cannot be fixed at the destination.

The Dayly Editors Published 24 July 2026 Updated 12 August 2026 3 min read
The short answer

Check passport expiry dates at least eight weeks before travel and confirm the validity rules for your destination — many require several months remaining. Keep originals and copies separately, and give older children their own documents to carry.

Everything else can be bought, borrowed or done without. This is the list where a mistake ends the trip before it starts.

Eight weeks before

  • Every passport’s expiry date, written down
  • The destination’s validity rule — many countries require three or six months’ validity
  • beyond your return date, and some count from entry rather than exit
  • Children’s passports are short — five years in many countries — and expire at inconvenient
  • moments
  • Blank pages, if you are travelling somewhere that stamps
  • Visa or entry authorisation requirements, which change and are worth re-checking even for
  • familiar destinations
  • Name mismatches — a parent with a different surname from a child is worth thinking about in
  • advance
Worth knowing

Passport renewal times vary and rise sharply at certain points in the year. If a passport expires within nine months of your travel date, renew it now rather than working out whether you technically need to.

Two weeks before

  • Booking confirmations — flights, accommodation, transfers, car hire
  • Travel insurance policy number and emergency contact
  • EHIC/GHIC or the equivalent health card
  • Driving licence and any international permit, if hiring a car
  • Any letter of consent, if one parent is travelling alone with children — some countries ask, and
  • it costs nothing to carry
  • Prescriptions, in original packaging, with a copy of the prescription
  • Vaccination records, if required
  • Accommodation address and phone number, written down and not only in an email

How to store them

Originals: one place, one bag, one adult. Not distributed across four pockets.

Copies: photographs of everything, stored somewhere accessible without a signal — downloaded, not just in cloud storage. Plus printed copies in a different bag from the originals.

Emergency: the accommodation address and one contact number on paper, in each adult’s wallet. Phones run out of battery at exactly the moment this matters.

What children carry

By age
Under 12Nothing official. An adult carries everything.
12–14Their own boarding pass, if they are old enough to keep track of it. Passports stay with an adult.
15+Their own passport and boarding pass, at least for part of the journey. Give them the responsibility before the trip where it matters.

The morning of

One person checks the documents. Out loud, against the list, before anyone gets in the car. Every passport in hand, counted.

Ninety seconds, and it is the last opportunity to catch the mistake that would otherwise be discovered at a desk.

What to try this week
  1. Write down every passport expiry date today.
  2. Check the destination’s validity rule, not just the expiry date.
  3. Photograph everything and download the images to a phone.
  4. Put the accommodation address on paper in a wallet.
