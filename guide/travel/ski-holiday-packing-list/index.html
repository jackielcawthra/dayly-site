Ski holiday packing list for families

The one holiday where forgetting a single item costs a day on the mountain.

The Dayly Editors Published 10 July 2026 Updated 12 August 2026 3 min read
The short answer

Pack layers rather than bulk, hire skis, boots and helmets for growing children, and check glove and thermal sizes eight weeks out. The items that ruin a ski day are gloves, goggles and socks — pack spares of all three.

Skiing is the least forgiving family holiday to pack for: the kit is specific, cold is unpleasant, and a missing glove costs a day.

On the mountain

  • Ski jacket and salopettes — waterproof, checked for fit
  • Thermal base layers — two sets per person
  • Mid layers — fleece or similar, two
  • Ski socks — one pair per day, long, and no ordinary socks
  • Gloves or mittens — plus a spare pair each, non-negotiable with children
  • Helmet — hire, unless you own one that still fits
  • Goggles — plus a spare or a cheap pair, they break and they fog
  • Neck warmer or buff
  • Sun cream, high factor, and lip balm with SPF
  • Small backpack for the mountain
  • Hand warmers, if you have young or cold children

Off the mountain

  • Snow boots — for walking around the resort
  • Warm coat that is not the ski jacket
  • Hat and normal gloves
  • Indoor clothes, warm
  • Swimming things — most resorts have a pool
  • Slippers or thick socks for apartments
  • One smarter outfit if you plan to eat out

Hire or buy

Hire: skis, boots and poles for everyone, and helmets for growing children. Children’s feet change every season and boots are the item where fit matters most and cost most.

Buy: thermals, socks, gloves, goggles, hats. Cheap, personal, and hard to hire well.

Borrow or buy second-hand: jackets and salopettes for children. They get one or two seasons out of them at most, and there is a good market.

Try this

Try every item of last year’s kit on eight weeks before you go. Children grow more than you think between winters, and gloves, boots and salopettes are the three that will not fit.

Documents and specifics

  • Passports, valid well beyond your return
  • Insurance that explicitly covers winter sports — standard travel insurance frequently does not
  • EHIC/GHIC or the equivalent for your country
  • Lift passes or booking confirmations
  • Ski school bookings and times
  • Hire shop confirmation, with heights, weights and shoe sizes for everyone

That last one is worth doing before you leave. Standing in a hire shop trying to remember four children’s shoe sizes is a specific kind of misery.

What children pack themselves

From about eight, everything on the “off the mountain” list plus their own thermals, socks and gloves. The technical items — helmet, goggles, jacket — are worth an adult check until about twelve, because the cost of the mistake is a lost day rather than a cold evening.

Their journey bag is entirely theirs at any age over five.

The three things people forget

Spare gloves. Wet gloves do not dry overnight in an apartment. Every child needs two pairs.

Sun cream. Snow reflects sunlight and children burn badly at altitude, including on cloudy days.

Ski socks. Ordinary socks inside ski boots cause blisters within an hour. One pair per day, and no exceptions.

What to try this week
  1. Try last year’s kit on now, eight weeks out.
  2. Check the insurance explicitly covers winter sports.
  3. Record everyone’s height, weight and shoe size for the hire shop before you leave.
  4. Buy the spare gloves.
