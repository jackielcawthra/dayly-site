Features

One place the whole family can read.

Dayly takes the information you already receive and turns it into a week everybody can see — the calendar, the jobs, the school things, the meals and the trips, each person seeing what is theirs.

How Dayly works

Stop being the only one who knows what’s happening.

1

Forward it

Send Dayly anything — an email, newsletter, PDF, photo or pasted text. You can even set up a rule so school and club emails go straight to Dayly.

2

Dayly reads it

Dayly finds the dates, times, places, kit and deadlines, and works out who it belongs to. You approve it before it goes into the calendar.

3

Everyone sees what’s theirs

One family calendar, with a colour for each person. Everyone sees their own plans, responsibilities and what’s coming up — without having to ask you.

See how it works, in detail →

The calendar

One week, everyone on it

Every person has a colour. Everyone sees the whole week and their own part of it — plans, practices, appointments, the day something is due. Children open it as themselves and see what belongs to them, not a list of instructions.

Forwarded post

A newsletter becomes a plan

Send an email, a PDF, a photograph of a letter or pasted text. Dayly finds the dates, the kit, the money and the deadlines, and works out whose they are. Nothing reaches the calendar until somebody taps to approve it, and every item stays linked to what it came from.

Responsibilities

Jobs that belong to a person

A job has an owner rather than a rotating column, so habits form and nobody has to check whose turn it is. Rooms can have someone who resets them, with an agreed standard. Rotation is available where it helps — weekly, fortnightly, termly — and off where it does not.

Who is here

The week knows who is away

Presence is recorded by half-day, because someone can be home for dinner and not for breakfast. Alternating weeks, one night a fortnight, a different pattern in the holidays. Nothing falls due on a day its owner is somewhere else, and the cooking rota skips them.

School

Kit days, deadlines and terms

What is needed tomorrow, what is due this week, when the term starts and ends. The things a school calendar never holds properly — a PE bag, a form, forty kronor — have somewhere to live and someone attached to them.

Meals and shopping

A cook rota and a list anyone can add to

Who is cooking, on which night, for how many people once you know who is home. The shopping list is one the children can write to as well, so the thing that ran out is not something one person has to notice.

Trips

A packing list per person

Each traveller gets their own list, which is what makes it possible to hand the packing over. A child ticks off their own; an adult keeps the passports, the insurance and the things that genuinely need an adult.

Together

Not a leaderboard

The household has one running total that everyone contributes to, and people can thank each other for something that helped. There are no streaks, no per-child ranking and nothing that turns a sibling into a competitor.

By design

What Dayly does not do to your family.

No location tracking

Dayly never knows where anybody is. It is not a gap we intend to close.

Nobody outside the household

No usernames, no friend requests, no way for a stranger to reach a child.

No monitoring view

A parent can see what is outstanding. There is no dashboard of a child’s failures.

Calendars export as a standard .ics feed. Everything else lives in Dayly, and leaves with you whenever you ask — see how we handle your family’s data.

The same questions we would ask if we were building your household by hand — about twenty minutes, and it produces a written brief you can work from.
