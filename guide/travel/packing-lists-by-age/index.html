Packing lists by age

What a child can pack, and what should stay yours, at every age.

The Dayly Editors Published 26 June 2026 Updated 12 August 2026 2 min read Ages 2–17
The short answer

Two- to four-year-olds carry a small bag with a toy and a snack; five- to seven-year-olds pack named items with an adult; eight- to eleven-year-olds pack their whole bag against a list; twelve and over pack independently and unchecked. Documents and medication stay on the adult list at every age.

What stays on the adult list, always

Whatever the child’s age: passports and documents, medication, insurance, money, the first aid kit, and anything the household shares. These are not learning opportunities.

2–4

They pack: nothing meaningfully. They can choose one toy and put it in a bag. They carry: a small backpack with a toy, a snack and a water bottle. On the adult list: everything, plus twice as much of everything absorbent as seems reasonable, and a change of clothes in the hand luggage.

5–7

They pack: named items, with an adult. “Put in five pairs of socks.” “Choose three tops.” They carry: their journey bag — book, drawing things, snack, water, headphones. Teach: counting against the list, laying things out before they go in.

8–11

They pack: their whole bag, against a written list, checked afterwards by an adult. They carry: their own case, and their own journey bag. Teach: thinking about the days — how long, what will we be doing, what will the weather be. Their list: tops, bottoms, underwear, socks, pyjamas, swimming things, one warm layer, shoes, toiletries, journey bag.

12–14

They pack: their whole bag, unchecked. They carry: everything of theirs, including responsibility for it at the airport. Teach: planning for specific activities rather than generic days, and what they will regret not bringing. New: their own toiletries, their own charger, their own travel documents in their own bag if they are old enough to be trusted with them.

15–17

They pack: everything. You do not see the bag. They carry: their own passport, boarding pass and money. New: they should be able to pack for a week away without a list, and increasingly to organise the trip itself.

Journey bag — every age over five

The most useful list in this article, because it prevents the specific misery of a long journey:

  • Book or comic
  • Headphones
  • Charger and battery pack
  • Water bottle, empty for security
  • Snacks
  • A layer, because vehicles are cold
  • Something to do that is not a screen
  • Any medication needed during the journey

Packed by them, carried by them, checked by nobody.

Try this

The night before, ask each child to name three things in their journey bag. The ones who cannot are the ones who have not packed it.

What to try this week
  1. Give each child the list for their age band, not the family list.
  2. Insist every child over five carries their own journey bag.
  3. Have them count against the list before it goes in the case.
