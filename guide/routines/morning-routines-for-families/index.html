Routines

Family morning routines

Mornings go wrong for four reasons, and three of them are solved the night before.

The Dayly Editors Published 20 February 2026 Updated 10 August 2026 3 min read Ages 3–16
The short answer

Work backwards from the leave time, prepare everything possible the night before, use a fixed six-step order posted where the children can see it, and remove screens from the morning entirely.

Four things cause most difficult mornings, and three of them happen the night before.

1. Nothing was prepared

Bags, uniform, kit, forms, lunches. Every one of these takes two minutes in the evening and ten in the morning, because in the morning you also have to find them.

2. There is no fixed order

Without one, every morning is improvised, and improvisation at 07:10 with three children produces exactly what you would expect.

Six steps: up, dressed, breakfast, teeth, bag, shoes. Same order daily. Posted at child height.

3. The leave time is fictional

Most families run on a leave time that has never actually worked. Time it honestly for a week — including coats, shoes, the thing that gets forgotten and the walk to the car — and use the real number.

4. There is a screen in it

The most reliable single improvement. Not because screens are evil, but because stopping them is the problem, and the stopping happens at the worst possible moment.

Try this

Time your actual morning for three days without changing anything. Most families discover the real duration is fifteen minutes longer than the one they plan around, which explains everything.

The shape of a good morning

The night before: bags packed, uniform out, kit checked, breakfast things out.

On waking: curtains open, same time daily, minimal conversation for five minutes.

The order: dressed before coming down works for most families — it removes the single hardest transition from the middle of the morning.

Breakfast: the same two or three options. Choice at breakfast is a decision nobody needs.

The last ten minutes: teeth, bag, shoes, coat, out. Nothing new introduced in this window, including conversation about the day ahead.

By age

By age
3–5A picture routine at their height. An adult narrates. Clothes laid out the night before, in order.
6–9A written list. Point at it rather than speaking. They dress themselves and put their own bag by the door.
10–13Their own alarm. They run the whole routine. You handle only the leave time.
14+Entirely theirs, including the consequence of being late.

The persistent problems

One child is much slower. Wake them ten minutes earlier, reduce their steps, give them the simplest breakfast. Do not try to make them faster; that is not a thing that works.

Nobody will get out of bed. Look at the bedtime, not the morning. A wake-up problem is almost always a sleep problem, and adolescents in particular need more than they get.

The last five minutes are chaos. Almost always because something was not prepared. Move it to the evening.

Everyone is shouting. Take the talking out. Point at the list. A morning with fewer words in it is better on every measure.

What to try this week
  1. Time your real morning for three days before changing anything.
  2. Move four things to the night before.
  3. Post the six-step order at child height.
  4. Remove screens from the morning for one week and compare.
