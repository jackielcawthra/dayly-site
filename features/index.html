How Dayly works

Stop being the only one who knows what’s happening.

1

Forward it

Send Dayly anything — an email, newsletter, PDF, photo or pasted text. You can even set up a rule so school and club emails go straight to Dayly.

2

Dayly reads it

Dayly finds the dates, times, places, kit and deadlines, and works out who it belongs to. You approve it before it goes into the calendar.

3

Everyone sees what’s theirs

One family calendar, with a colour for each person. Everyone sees their own plans, responsibilities and what’s coming up — without having to ask you.

See how it works, in detail →

Stop being the only one who knows

Right now the letters, the emails and the messages land on one person, and everyone else finds out by asking them. Forward what arrives and Dayly turns it into a week the whole family can read — so nobody has to be the person who remembers.

Stop reminding them about everything

Children see their own things, in their own words, on their own screen. The prompting moves from your voice to something they can look at, which is the only way it ever stops being your voice.

Get them to do their share, without a fight

A job belongs to a person, not to a rota nobody can remember. Nothing to negotiate each day, because there is nothing left to decide.

Stop forgetting the kit day and the form

The PE bag, the signed slip, the forty kronor, the deadline three weeks out. All the things a calendar has nowhere to put, with a date and a person attached.

Share it properly with the other adult

Not a list you delegate from — the same week, visible to both of you, so the second parent can genuinely see what needs doing instead of being told.

Get through the morning without a scramble

Tomorrow, tonight: what is happening, what is needed and who is where, while there is still time to do something about it.

Raise children who can run their own lives

The point is not a tidier house this week. It is a fifteen-year-old who notices what needs doing, because they have been noticing since they were six.

Stop it feeling like nagging

Everyone adds to one household total, and anyone can thank somebody for something that helped. No streaks, no leaderboards, and no ranking one child against another.

What you set up

Built around your household, not a template.

Create the calendar your life actually runs on

School newsletters, WhatsApp messages, club emails, a photo of the letter that came home in a bag. Forward any of it and Dayly turns it into dated entries against the right person — you tap to approve before anything lands.

Organise your whole life in one place

The calendar, the jobs, school, meals, shopping and trips — not six apps and a list on the fridge. One week, one place, everyone on it.

Teach your children to take responsibility

Jobs that belong to a person rather than a rota, ticked off by them, on their own screen. The aim is a fifteen-year-old who notices what needs doing without being asked.

Move the mental load onto the whole family

The noticing, the remembering and the planning stop living in one head. Everyone can see what is coming, so nobody has to be the person who holds it.

Build your own list of jobs, and what each is worth

Your words, your household. Write the jobs that actually exist here, group them how you think about them — the kitchen, the mornings, the pets — and decide what each one is worth.

See who did what, and earn points together

Every job ticked off is logged against whoever did it, so you can see participation across the week. Points go to one household total — earned by doing your own jobs and by thanking somebody else for theirs.

Work towards a reward as a household

Set a target and what everyone gets when the total reaches it. There is no winner and nobody is behind, which is the point.

Plan the week’s meals

Who is cooking, on which night, for how many — the rota already knows who is home. What the week needs goes straight onto the shopping list.

Share one shopping list

Everyone in the house can add to it, including the children, so noticing that something ran out stops being one person’s job.

Sort the logistics before they become a problem

Who needs to be where, who is taking them, and what time you actually have to leave — worked out from the day itself rather than guessed at while everyone stands by the door.

Stop having to remember any of it

Nothing depends on somebody keeping it in their head. It is written down, dated, attached to a person, and linked back to the letter it came from if you ever need to check.

Handle two homes without two systems

Presence is recorded half-day by half-day, so alternating weeks and one night a fortnight just work. Nothing falls due on a day its owner is somewhere else.

Give a room an owner

Somebody keeps the hallway, with an agreed standard and a record of when it was last reset. Rotate it termly, or never.

Pack a trip between everybody

Each traveller gets their own list and ticks off their own. You keep the passports, the insurance and the things that genuinely need an adult.

By design

What Dayly does not do to your family.

No location tracking

Dayly never knows where anybody is. It is not a gap we intend to close.

Nobody outside the household

No usernames, no friend requests, no way for a stranger to reach a child.

No monitoring view

A parent can see what is outstanding. There is no dashboard of a child’s failures.

Calendars export as a standard .ics feed. Everything else lives in Dayly, and leaves with you whenever you ask — see how we handle your family’s data.

The same questions we would ask if we were building your household by hand — about twenty minutes, and it produces a written brief you can work from.
