School morning routines that actually work

A calm morning is mostly built the evening before.

The Dayly Editors Published 5 August 2026 Updated 14 August 2026 3 min read Ages 4–16
The short answer

Work backwards from the actual leave time rather than forwards from waking, move everything that can be done the night before, use a fixed order of five or six steps, and replace verbal prompts with a visible list. Screens before school reliably make mornings worse.

Work backwards, not forwards

Most families plan mornings forwards from waking up and discover the gap at the door. Do it the other way:

  1. When does the first child need to be somewhere? That is the fixed point.
  2. Subtract travel time, honestly, including parking and the walk.
  3. Subtract five minutes of coats and shoes, which always takes five minutes.
  4. That is the leave time. Everything else fits before it or does not happen.

Write the leave time on the wall. Not the getting-up time — the leave time. It is the only number that matters and it is the one families argue about at 07:50.

Move it to the night before

Everything that can be done the evening before should be:

  • Bags packed and by the door
  • Uniform out, including socks and shoes
  • Kit checked against tomorrow’s list
  • Forms signed, money in the bag
  • Breakfast things out, if that helps
  • Anything that needs charging, charging

This is the highest-leverage change available and it takes five minutes. The evening check.

A fixed order

The same sequence every morning, small enough to fit on a card:

  1. Up and dressed
  2. Breakfast
  3. Teeth and hair
  4. Bag by the door
  5. Shoes and coat
  6. Leave

Order removes decisions. Decisions are what nobody has capacity for at 07:15.

Try this

Post the list at child height by the door. Then, for one week, point at it instead of speaking. Most parents are startled by how much of the morning was their voice.

The screen question

The single most reliable improvement most families make: no screens before school. Not as a punishment — because stopping is the problem. Every morning that contains a screen contains an argument about turning it off, and it arrives at exactly the worst moment.

If it is currently allowed, expect a rough week when it stops, and a better month after.

Getting them up

  • Same time every day, including a reasonable approximation at weekends. Wildly different weekend
  • timings make Monday worse.
  • Light helps, especially in winter. Open the curtains before speaking.
  • Their own alarm from about nine. It moves the responsibility and removes you from the first
  • interaction of the day.
  • Do not negotiate the first five minutes. Wake, light, out. Discussion at that moment is a trap.

When one child is much slower

Common, and usually not deliberate. Options that work better than hurrying them:

  • Wake them ten minutes earlier than everyone else
  • Reduce their steps by moving more to the night before
  • Give them the simplest breakfast
  • Let them be dressed before they come down, or after — whichever suits them, consistently
What to try this week
  1. Calculate the real leave time backwards and write it on the wall.
  2. Move five things to the night before.
  3. Put the six-step list at child height by the door.
  4. Try one week with no screens before school.

Common questions

What time should children get up for school?

Work backwards from your leave time and allow about an hour for primary-age children and forty-five minutes for teenagers, assuming bags are packed the night before. Then protect the bedtime that makes that wake-up survivable — that is the actual lever.
