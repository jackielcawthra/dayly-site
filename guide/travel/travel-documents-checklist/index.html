Family travel documents checklist

There are plenty of things you can buy when you arrive. A missing passport is not one of them.

The Dayly Editors Published 24 July 2026 Updated 22 August 2026 3 min read Ages 0–18
The short answer

Check every passport expiry date eight weeks out, and check the validity rules for your destination rather than assuming — many require months remaining beyond the trip. Keep originals together and copies separately, write the address on paper, and count the passports before anyone gets into the car.

There are plenty of things you can buy when you arrive. A missing passport is not one of them.

Travel documents are the part of family travel worth checking early, because a small mistake can become a very large problem at the airport. Passport expiry dates, entry requirements and consent rules are all things that are much easier to sort out weeks before departure than on the morning you leave.

Eight weeks before

  • Check every passport expiry date.
  • Check the entry and passport-validity rules for your destination. Some countries require several months of validity beyond the trip.
  • Check whether you need a visa or other entry authorisation.
  • Check that passports have enough blank pages if your destination requires them.
  • Check names carefully, particularly if a parent and child have different surnames.
  • Check children’s passports especially carefully. They can expire sooner than adults’ passports.

If a passport expires within the next year, it is worth checking the rules now rather than assuming it will be fine.

Two weeks before

Gather the documents and information you are likely to need:

  • Flight, accommodation and transfer confirmations
  • Travel insurance details and emergency contact number
  • EHIC/GHIC or the relevant health card
  • Driving licence and international permit, if required
  • Consent letter if one parent is travelling alone with the children, where relevant
  • Prescriptions and any required medical documentation
  • Vaccination records if required
  • Accommodation address and phone number

Keep the accommodation address somewhere you can access without opening an email or relying on a good internet connection.

Keep originals and copies separately

Keep the original passports and essential documents together rather than distributing them between several bags.

Then make copies or photographs of the important documents and save them somewhere accessible offline. Printed copies in a separate bag are useful too.

It is worth having the accommodation address and one emergency contact written on paper. Phones are wonderfully convenient until the battery is dead.

What should children carry?

Children can gradually take responsibility for some of their own travel documents, but this should grow with their ability to manage them.

A younger child does not need to carry their passport simply to prove that they are independent. An older child may be perfectly capable of keeping track of a boarding pass or looking after their own travel information.

The principle is the same as with other responsibilities: give children something real to own, and increase it as they are ready.

For a teenager, that might mean keeping their boarding pass, checking their own travel information and eventually carrying their passport for part of the journey.

The point is not to make children responsible for something important before they are ready. It is to give them practice before they genuinely need the skill.

The final check

On the morning you leave, one adult should check the documents against the list before everyone gets into the car.

Every passport. Counted.

It takes very little time, and it catches the kind of mistake that is particularly difficult to fix once you have reached the airport.

Family travel does involve a certain amount of adult responsibility. But it does not all have to stay there. As children grow, they can gradually take ownership of more of the information and belongings that belong to them.

That is part of the holiday too: not just getting everyone there, but giving children some experience of getting themselves there.

What to try this week
  1. Write down every passport expiry date today.
  2. Check the destination’s validity rule, not just the expiry date.
  3. Photograph everything and download the images to a phone.
  4. Put the accommodation address on paper in a wallet.
