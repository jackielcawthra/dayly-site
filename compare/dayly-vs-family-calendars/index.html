Dayly vs a dedicated family calendar app

The closest category to us, and the one where the differences are most worth spelling out.

Written by the people who make Dayly. We have an obvious interest, so we have tried to say clearly where the alternative wins and who should not use ours.

What family calendar apps do well

  • Multiple people in one view, colour-coded, which a generic calendar handles poorly
  • Shared lists alongside the calendar
  • Syncing with the calendars people already use, often better than we do
  • Simple setup — most take fifteen minutes
  • Widgets and displays designed for a kitchen screen

If your problem is “we cannot see each other’s week”, these solve it, and several are very good.

Where the categories diverge

Recurring responsibility. A calendar app models events. It generally cannot express “the hallway is Noah’s until 1 May, then Emma’s” — an ownership that rotates, with a standard attached and a record of when it was last done.

Presence. Half-day accurate presence, and jobs that do not fall due when their owner is away, is unusual outside apps built for families that move between homes.

A genuine child view. Most family calendar apps offer a filtered view, which is not the same as a screen designed for a nine-year-old: what is permanently yours, what is due tonight, what tomorrow needs, in their words.

The mental load framing. Dayly is built around moving responsibility from parent to child over time. That produces different decisions — no leaderboards, no streaks, no monitoring view, points scoped to genuinely extra effort only.

Where they beat us

Calendar sync. If live two-way sync with Google or Apple is essential, several competitors do it today and we do not.

Speed of setup. Dayly takes about an hour because there is more to say. A calendar app is quicker.

Kitchen display hardware. If you want a wall-mounted screen in the kitchen, look at the products built around that.

How to choose

Write down what actually went wrong in your house in the last month. If most of it was “we did not know about that”, a family calendar app is the right tool. If most of it was “I was the only one who knew about that”, the difference is ownership rather than visibility, and that is what we are for.

Common questions

Do you have a kitchen display mode?

There is a week view that works well on a spare tablet, but we do not make hardware and we are not optimised for a wall-mounted screen. If that is the main thing you want, buy from someone who is.

Other comparisons

Dayly vs a shared calendar

If your only problem is double-booking, stop reading and set up a shared calendar. It is free and it works.

Dayly vs a chore chart on the fridge

A chart on a wall has one enormous advantage over any app: it is always visible and needs no decision to open.

Dayly vs the family WhatsApp group

Almost every family runs partly on WhatsApp. It is a superb messenger and a terrible system of record.

Dayly vs a paper family planner

There is a reason the wall planner keeps selling. It is visible without anyone deciding to look at it.
