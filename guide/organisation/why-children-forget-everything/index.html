Why children forget everything

You told them twice this morning. By the door, it was gone. Here is what was actually happening.

The Dayly Editors Published 12 March 2026 Updated 6 August 2026 4 min read
The short answer

Children forget because working memory is limited and still developing, because verbal instructions vanish the moment they are said, because nothing in the environment triggers the memory at the right time, and because a reliable adult memory makes their own unnecessary. Each has a fix, and none of them is repeating yourself.

“I told him twice.”

Yes. And telling is the least reliable way there is to install a memory in a child.

Here is what is actually going on, in four parts.

1. Working memory is small, and still growing

Working memory is the mental notepad. The thing that holds an instruction while you act on it.

In children it is genuinely limited. Most seven-year-olds can hold roughly two or three items reliably, and the capacity keeps growing into adolescence (Gathercole et al., 2004).

“Get your shoes on, put your reading folder in your bag and bring down your washing” is three items. Delivered while they are doing something else.

One or two will survive.

The fix: one instruction at a time. Or a list.

2. Spoken instructions disappear

Speech has no persistence.

The moment it is finished, it exists only in the child’s memory — which is the least reliable place available.

Anything written, drawn or physically placed can be checked a second time.

The fix: put it somewhere they can look at twice.

3. Nothing prompts it at the right moment

Most childhood forgetting is not a failure to know.

It is a failure to remember at the moment it matters. Psychologists call that prospective memory, and it is genuinely hard for children.

Your child knows it is swimming today. They just did not think about it while standing next to their bag.

The fix: put the reminder in their path. The kit by the door. The list where the bag lives. The note on the shoes.

A prompt in the environment beats a prompt at breakfast.

Try this

Instead of telling them at breakfast, put the object somewhere they will physically trip over it.

Most “forgetful” children stop forgetting when the cue is in the right place at the right time.

4. Somebody else is remembering

The most overlooked reason. And the one you can do something about.

A child with a reliable adult memory nearby has no need of their own.

That is not laziness. It is efficiency. Adults do exactly the same thing with a partner who happens to be good at birthdays.

The fix: stop being the memory, and give them something else to check.

Why it gets worse at thirteen

Parents often notice a distinct dip in early adolescence. Sometimes after years of steady improvement.

The current understanding is that the prefrontal regions supporting planning and self-control are being substantially reorganised through the teens, and performance can wobble in the middle of that.

Meanwhile the demands on exactly those systems increase sharply.

So expect a dip somewhere between twelve and fourteen. And do not withdraw the external structure just because they seemed to have outgrown it at eleven.

When to ask somebody

Everyday forgetting is normal.

It is worth raising with school or your GP if the forgetting is:

  • Much more extreme than other children of the same age, in every setting
  • Alongside real difficulty starting or finishing anything at all
  • Causing distress, or damaging school or friendships
  • Not improving at all over a year, despite good external structure

None of those is a diagnosis.

They are reasons to ask somebody who can actually assess your child.

What to try this week
  1. Cut multi-part instructions to one thing at a time.
  2. Write down the three things you say most often and put them where they can be seen.
  3. Move one cue into the physical path — the kit by the door, the list on the bag.
  4. Notice how often you are the trigger, and replace yourself with an object.

Common questions

Why can they remember every detail of a game and not their PE kit?

Because interest drives memory, and prospective memory — remembering to do something at a future moment — is a different and much harder task than recalling something you care about. It is not selective in the sense of deliberate.

Sources & further reading

  1. Gathercole, S. et al. (2004). The structure of working memory from 4 to 15 years of age. Developmental Psychology
  2. Diamond, A. (2013). Executive Functions. Annual Review of Psychology
