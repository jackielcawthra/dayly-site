How to remember school deadlines

Deadlines are not missed because they were forgotten. They were never written down.

The Dayly Editors Published 8 August 2026 Updated 14 August 2026 2 min read
The short answer

Capture every deadline at the moment you hear about it, with the date it is due and the date you need to act. For anything requiring preparation, put a reminder several days before the deadline rather than on it — the deadline itself is too late to be useful.

There are two dates for every school deadline and families usually record one of them.

The due date. When the thing has to be in. The act date. When you have to start, buy, sign or find the thing in order to make the due date.

Recording only the due date is why you find out about the pirate costume at nine o’clock the night before World Book Day, having known about it for a month.

The capture habit

At the moment you hear about anything with a date:

  1. Put the due date in the calendar, with the child’s name
  2. Put the act date in the calendar too — two days before for a form, a week before for anything
  3. that needs buying, two weeks for a costume
  4. If it needs a thing, put the thing on the shopping list now
  5. If it needs a decision, note what has to be decided

Thirty seconds, once. The alternative is holding it for three weeks and then not.

Try this

Go through your school app, the class chat and the fridge right now and find every date in the next six weeks. Add the act date for each one. This takes about fifteen minutes and it is the highest-value quarter-hour in the school year.

The categories that get missed

Costumes and dress-up days. Always more work than the notice implies. Act date: two weeks.

Money in an envelope. Nobody carries cash now. Act date: the day you hear about it.

Trips with forms. The form and the payment are frequently separate deadlines with separate consequences.

Things needed for a lesson. A shoebox, a photograph, an empty jar, a family recipe. Small, easy and invisible until the morning.

Projects. Need decomposition, not a reminder.

Sign-up windows. Clubs, options, trips, parents’ evening slots. These close, and the consequence of missing them is not recoverable.

Handing it to the children

From about eleven, a child can hold their own deadlines with a weekly check. From fourteen, without one.

The transition is a weekly two-minute conversation: what have you got coming up, and when do you need to start? Not a check of whether they have done it — a prompt to look ahead. Then stop asking, in stages.

What to try this week
  1. Find every school date in the next six weeks and record both dates for each.
  2. Add the act date, not just the due date.
  3. Put anything that needs buying on the list today.
  4. Start a two-minute weekly look-ahead with older children.
