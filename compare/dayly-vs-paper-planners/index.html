Dayly vs a paper family planner

There is a reason the wall planner keeps selling. It is visible without anyone deciding to look at it.

Written by the people who make Dayly. We have an obvious interest, so we have tried to say clearly where the alternative wins and who should not use ours.

What paper does better

Passive visibility. The single biggest advantage. Nobody has to open anything; it is simply there in the kitchen, seen forty times a day.

No adoption problem. Grandparents, babysitters and reluctant teenagers can all read a wall.

Writing things down helps you remember them, which is a real and well-documented effect.

It is pleasant. People genuinely enjoy a paper planner in a way they do not enjoy software, and that matters more than it should.

Where it stops working

One copy, one place. The information is in your kitchen. You are at work, at the school gate, in a shop, on the phone to a coach.

Rewriting. Anything recurring has to be written again every week, every month, every year. That is work, and it is held by one person.

No presence. If children are not at home every day, most weeks the grid is wrong.

No record. When was the bathroom last done, who cooked on Tuesday, has the form been sent.

One person maintains it. Which is the thing you were trying to solve. A planner that only one person writes on is a planner that only one person is responsible for.

The combination we would actually suggest

Paper for the week ahead, on a wall, where everyone walks past it. Software for the system underneath: the recurring, the owned, the school information, the deadlines further out, the trips.

Dayly prints a week, so the wall version can be generated rather than rewritten. That removes the main cost of paper while keeping its main advantage.

Who should stay on paper

Families with young children and a simple, stable week. There is no reason to introduce software into a system that is working, and “it would be nice to have it on my phone” is not a strong enough reason to give one person a maintenance job.

If your only problem is double-booking, stop reading and set up a shared calendar. It is free and it works.

A chart on a wall has one enormous advantage over any app: it is always visible and needs no decision to open.

Dayly vs the family WhatsApp group

Almost every family runs partly on WhatsApp. It is a superb messenger and a terrible system of record.

Dayly vs a general task app

Task managers assume a self-directed adult with a laptop. That describes about half of one person in most households.
