Dayly vs the family WhatsApp group

Almost every family runs partly on WhatsApp. It is a superb messenger and a terrible system of record.

Written by the people who make Dayly. We have an obvious interest, so we have tried to say clearly where the alternative wins and who should not use ours.

What WhatsApp is good at

Everyone already has it. No adoption problem, which is a genuine and underrated advantage.

Instant coordination. “Running 10 minutes late”, “can you get milk”, “who has the car”.

The class group. Where you find out things the school did not send, and where somebody eventually asks the question everyone was hoping somebody would ask.

Photos of letters, which is how a substantial share of school information now travels.

Why nothing survives in it

It is a stream, not a store. A date mentioned on Tuesday is forty messages back by Thursday and unfindable by the following week.

Search is bad for this. You are trying to remember whether it was the class group, the football group or a message from one parent, and what word to search for.

No dates, no owners, no structure. A message saying “PE has moved to Wednesday” does not change anything anywhere.

It requires relaying. One adult reads it and tells the other. That is a job, and it is a job held by the person who was already carrying everything.

Children cannot use it as a source. A nine-year-old cannot check the family group to find out what they need on Thursday.

The practical answer

Keep WhatsApp for messages. Treat it as an inbox, never as a store.

The rule that fixes most of it: when something arrives, it goes into the family system before you put the phone down. A date onto the calendar. A form into the tray. A thing to buy onto the list. Thirty seconds, at the moment of arrival.

Anything that stays in the chat has to be remembered by a person, and that person is you.

What Dayly adds

A place for the information to land: dates with owners, kit days that recur, forms with a deadline, and a view for each child so nobody has to be told.

It does not replace the group chat, and it does not want to. It replaces the part where you scroll back through it on a Wednesday night looking for a date.

Common questions

Should we stop using the class WhatsApp group?

No — it is genuinely useful, and it is where a lot of real information appears first. Just do not treat it as a place you can find something again.

A chart on a wall has one enormous advantage over any app: it is always visible and needs no decision to open.

Dayly vs a paper family planner

There is a reason the wall planner keeps selling. It is visible without anyone deciding to look at it.

Dayly vs a general task app

Task managers assume a self-directed adult with a laptop. That describes about half of one person in most households.
