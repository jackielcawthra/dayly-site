What should children pack themselves? A guide by age

Travel is a useful place to practise responsibility because the jobs are concrete. There is a bag, a list and a clear reason for the things in it.

The Dayly Editors Published 26 June 2026 Updated 22 August 2026 4 min read Ages 2–17
The short answer

Two- to four-year-olds choose and carry; five- to seven-year-olds pack named items from a list; eight- to eleven-year-olds pack the whole bag and an adult checks it; from twelve it is increasingly theirs. Documents, medication and money stay with the adult at every age. The journey bag is the easiest thing to hand over first.

Travel is a useful place to practise responsibility because the jobs are concrete. There is a bag, a list and a clear reason for having the things in it.

The aim is not to make a child responsible for everything at once. It is to let responsibility grow with their ability, while keeping the things that genuinely need an adult’s attention with the adult.

What stays with the adult

Some things should not become a learning exercise.

Passports, important travel documents, medication, insurance, money and shared family items remain the adult’s responsibility unless an older child is genuinely ready to manage part of them safely.

Independence does not mean handing over important things before a child is ready.

Ages 2–4: choosing and carrying

At this age, packing is mostly about participation.

A child can choose a favourite toy, help put it in a small bag and carry that bag themselves. They can begin to understand that when something belongs to them, they have a part in looking after it.

The adult still does the actual packing.

Ages 5–7: packing with help

Children can start packing specific things from a simple list.

“Put in five pairs of socks” is easier to understand than “pack your clothes.” They can lay things out, count them and put them into the bag themselves.

They can also take responsibility for their journey bag: a book, snack, water bottle, headphones and something to do.

An adult can check the important things afterwards. The child is practising the process, not yet carrying the whole responsibility.

Ages 8–11: the whole bag

This is a good age for children to pack their own main bag against a written list.

The list can include clothes, underwear, pyjamas, swimming things, shoes and toiletries. The child can think about how long the trip is, what activities are planned and what the weather will be like.

An adult can check the finished bag, particularly for important or easily forgotten items.

The useful part is not whether they packed perfectly. It is that they are beginning to think ahead rather than waiting for someone else to tell them what they need.

Ages 12–14: increasing ownership

At this age, many children can take much more responsibility for their own packing.

Instead of being given every item, they can work out what they need for the trip and use a list to check themselves.

They can also take responsibility for their own toiletries, chargers and personal belongings.

Whether they are ready to manage travel documents or money themselves is a separate question. That depends on the child, the journey and the level of trust involved.

Teenagers: preparing for life without you

Older teenagers should increasingly be able to pack for a week away without a parent standing over the bag.

They can think through what they will need, check the weather, consider the activities and make decisions about what to bring. They can also learn to look after their own travel information and belongings when they are ready.

You do not need to inspect every bag forever.

At some point, the responsibility has to become genuinely theirs.

The journey bag is a good place to start

A small travel bag is one of the easiest responsibilities to hand over because the contents are predictable.

It might contain:

  • A book or something to do
  • Headphones
  • Charger
  • Water bottle
  • Snacks
  • A warm layer
  • Something that does not involve a screen
  • Any medication they are responsible for during the journey

Let the child pack it and carry it.

If something small is forgotten, they have a chance to notice what happens and remember it next time.

The age is only a guide

Children do not become responsible according to a birthday.

One eight-year-old may be perfectly capable of packing independently while another still needs help. A teenager who is travelling somewhere unfamiliar may need more support than they would for a weekend with grandparents.

The question is less “What should a twelve-year-old be able to do?” and more ”What part of this can my child genuinely own now?”

Then give them that part.

Over time, the list gets bigger, the checking gets lighter and the adult becomes less involved.

That is the point of teaching children to pack for themselves. Not a perfectly packed suitcase, but a child who is gradually learning how to prepare for their own life.

What to try this week
  1. Give each child the list for their age band, not the family list.
  2. Insist every child over five carries their own journey bag.
  3. Have them count against the list before it goes in the case.
