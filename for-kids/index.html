How Dayly works

Stop being the only one who knows what’s happening.

1

Forward it

Send Dayly anything — an email, newsletter, PDF, photo or pasted text. You can even set up a rule so school and club emails go straight to Dayly.

2

Dayly reads it

Dayly finds the dates, times, places, kit and deadlines, and works out who it belongs to. You approve it before it goes into the calendar.

3

Everyone sees what’s theirs

One family calendar, with a colour for each person. Everyone sees their own plans, responsibilities and what’s coming up — without having to ask you.

What a child sees

Four things, in their own words.

You’re in charge of

The permanent things. Their bag, their kit, the area of the house that is theirs — and until when, if it rotates.

Your days

The rota, with today marked. Whose turn it is to cook, who is emptying the dishwasher, whose washing day it is.

Waiting on you

What is due and has not happened. Not framed as failure — just what is still outstanding.

Tomorrow

What is on, what to pack, what needs an adult to know something. Checked the night before, when there is still time.

By age

It grows with them.

The same app looks different at six and at sixteen, because what a child can hold changes. You add the next thing when the last one has gone quiet.

4–6Pictures, two or three steps, the same order every day. Shoes, coat, one job at the table.
7–10Their bag and their kit, packed the night before against their own list. One area of the house with their name on it.
11–13Their whole week: deadlines, kit, plans, their own washing day. They see clashes before they happen.
14–17Their diary, their appointments, their revision plan, their packing. You stop being the reminder service.

What it is not

This is not a monitoring app.

  • No location tracking.Dayly does not know where your child is and does not want to.
  • No leaderboards.Siblings are not ranked. Nobody is losing every day.
  • No streaks to break.A missed week costs nothing. Systems that punish gaps get abandoned in the weeks families most need them.
  • No advertising, ever.Nothing is sold to your child and nothing about them is sold to anyone else.
  • No reporting on them.A parent can see what is outstanding. There is no dashboard of a child’s failures.
A child’s view belongs to the child. That is the whole point of giving them one.

How we handle children’s data

Teenagers

The version that works at fifteen.

It is theirs, not a lead

No tracking, no monitoring, no reporting. If it reads as surveillance, a teenager will not use it, and they will be right.

Only what concerns them

They do not need the household admin. They need their week, their deadlines, their kit and their money.

It does not nag

No notifications by default. Being reminded by an app is not much better than being reminded by a parent.
