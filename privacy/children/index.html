Privacy

How children’s information is handled

Dayly is for families, which means children’s information is in it. This page sets out exactly what that means — what is stored, who can see it, and what we will never do.

The short version

A child in Dayly has a first name, a colour, the days they are here, and their own responsibilities. That is essentially it. There is no email address, no phone number, no location, no advertising, no profiling, and nothing leaves the household.

What is stored about a child

  • A first name or nickname, chosen by the household
  • A colour and initials, for the avatar
  • Which days and half-days they are present, if that varies
  • Their responsibilities, jobs, areas and routines
  • Their school information, if the household enters it: term dates, kit days, timetable, deadlines
  • What they have ticked off, and when
  • Points, if the household uses them

What is never stored

  • Email address or phone number — a child opens Dayly with a code, not an account
  • Location, at any time
  • Photographs, unless a household chooses to add one, which is optional and stays in the household
  • Anything that identifies a child outside their own home
  • Health or special-category data

Who can see it

The child sees their own view: what is theirs, what is due, what tomorrow needs.

The adults in the household see everything the household has entered, because that is what a shared household system is.

Other children in the household see the shared things — the week, meals, the shopping list — and not another child’s private view. A child cannot switch to a sibling’s profile.

Nobody else. Not us, except where an engineer needs access to fix a specific fault, which is logged. Not advertisers. Not schools. Not other families.

What a parent cannot do

Deliberately, there is no surveillance view. No location history, no screen time reporting, no log of what a child looked at, no dashboard of their failures. A parent can see what is outstanding — that is the point of a household list — and nothing beyond it.

We think an app that lets a parent monitor a child is a different product, and that it works against what Dayly is for. A child who experiences the app as surveillance will not use it, and they would be right not to.

Messaging and contact

There is no messaging with anyone outside the household. No friend requests, no public profiles, no usernames, no discoverability. A child in Dayly cannot be contacted by a stranger, because there is no mechanism through which a stranger could exist.

Advertising and profiling

Never. Not now, and not as a future business model. No advertising SDKs, no behavioural profiling, no data sold or shared for marketing. This is a paid product precisely so that it does not have to be either of those things.

Codes rather than passwords

Each child has a short code that opens Dayly as them. This is a deliberate trade: it is simple enough that a six-year-old can use it, and it is not strong authentication. It keeps a sibling out of another sibling’s view; it is not a defence against a determined person who already has the device.

We would rather say that plainly than imply security we do not provide. Households wanting stronger separation should use device-level protection as well.

Deletion

An adult can remove a person from the household at any time, and that removes their information. To delete the entire household, write to hello@dayly.app — we will do it, permanently, and confirm.

Legal basis

Where a household enters information about children, the adult account holder is providing that information as the child’s parent or guardian, and we process it to provide the service they have asked for. Dayly is intended to be set up and controlled by an adult.

Common questions

What age is Dayly suitable for?

Children as young as four can use a picture-based view with an adult nearby. From about seven, most children use their own view independently. There is no minimum, because the household controls what each child sees.

Can I stop my child seeing certain things?

Yes. Notes and information can be marked adults-only, and they do not appear under a child’s profile at all.

Is the child code secure?

It is a curtain, not a lock — enough to keep a sibling out, not enough to stop someone who has the unlocked device. We say this plainly rather than overstating it.
