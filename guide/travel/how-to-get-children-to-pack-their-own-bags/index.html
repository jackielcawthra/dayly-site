How to get children to pack their own bags

The first trip where they packed badly is worth more than five where you packed well.

The Dayly Editors Published 12 June 2026 Updated 12 August 2026 3 min read Ages 5–17
The short answer

Give the child a written list in their own words, pack together first, then supervise, then check afterwards, then stop checking. Pack three days before departure so gaps can be fixed, and hold the rule that what is not packed does not come.

Packing is one of the easiest responsibilities to hand over, because the list is finite, the deadline is known and the consequence is instructive without being serious.

Pack three days early

Not the night before. Three days before gives room for the discovery that the swimming costume no longer fits — a discovery which, made the night before, becomes an adult’s problem at ten o’clock.

The list

Written, theirs, by category rather than by day for younger children:

  • Tops — one per day plus one
  • Bottoms — one per two days
  • Underwear and socks — one per day plus two
  • Pyjamas
  • Swimming things
  • One warm layer
  • Shoes: worn, plus one pair
  • Toiletries
  • Journey bag: book, headphones, charger, water

For a specific trip, add the specific things: ski gloves, walking boots, a smart outfit, goggles.

Try this

Have them lay everything out on the bed before it goes in the bag, and count the piles against the list. Counting is much easier than remembering, and it is what catches the missing pyjamas.

The four stages

1. Together. You pack alongside them, saying what you are thinking. Ages five to seven.

2. They pack, you supervise. Present, asking questions, supplying no answers. Eight to ten.

3. They pack, you check afterwards. Quietly, and you mention only what is genuinely serious. Eleven to twelve.

4. They pack. No check at all. Thirteen and up.

Move up a stage each trip if the last one went reasonably. Most children can reach stage four by about twelve.

The rule

What is not packed does not come.

Which means holding it. A child who arrives without pyjamas sleeps in a t-shirt, and packs pyjamas for the rest of their life. This is a cheap lesson and it does not work if you carry a shadow bag of the things they forgot.

Two sensible exceptions: medication, and anything that would genuinely ruin the trip for other people. Those go on the adult’s list, not the child’s.

What they carry

Children can carry their own bag from about five — a small one, wheeled or on their back. This is worth insisting on, because a child carrying their own bag makes different packing decisions than one whose bag is carried for them.

Coming home

Same rule, in reverse, and it is the half that never gets taught. They pack their own bag to come home, they check the room, and they empty the bag when you get back. A child who unpacks knows where their things are for the next trip.

What to try this week
  1. Write the list with them, in their words, before the next trip.
  2. Pack three days early, not the night before.
  3. Move up exactly one stage from where you actually are.
  4. Decide in advance that you will not carry a shadow bag.
