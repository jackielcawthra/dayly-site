How it works

An hour to set up. Then it runs.

Most family apps ask you to keep them fed. This one asks for one evening at the start, and after that it works from what already arrives in your inbox.

The first evening

1

Put the household in

Names, ages and a colour each. Ten minutes.

If two people share custody, say which weeks — Dayly works in half-days, so “home for dinner, away at breakfast” is a thing it understands.

2

Say who does what

Not a full rota. Three or four things each that are genuinely theirs.

You can add the rest over the following weeks, and most families do.

3

Forward one thing

A newsletter, a club email, a photo of a letter. See what comes back before you commit to anything.

That first one is usually the moment it makes sense.

If you would rather be asked the questions than stare at an empty screen, the setup quiz does exactly that — it is the same list we would work through with you, and it takes about twenty minutes.

After that

What a normal week actually involves.

Very little, which is the point. Here is the whole of it.

Forwarding, a few seconds

Something arrives, you send it on and put your phone away. You do not read it properly, summarise it or copy anything across.

Approving, about a minute

Dayly shows you what it found and who it thinks it belongs to. You tap to accept, or fix the bit it got wrong. Nothing reaches the calendar until you do.

Looking, once a day

Usually in the morning, sometimes the night before. Everyone else does the same on their own screen, which is the part that removes the asking.

Being straight about it

What happens when it gets something wrong.

It will. A newsletter that says “week beginning the 14th” without a year, a fixture list where the ages are in a column heading, a letter photographed at an angle in bad light.

Three things follow from that, and they are the reason the product is shaped the way it is.

Nothing lands without a tap. However confident the reading, a guardian approves it. That is deliberate, and it is not a setting.

Every item keeps its source. If a date looks wrong in three weeks, you can open the letter it came from rather than trying to remember.

It says when it is unsure. A thing it half-understood is flagged as a question, not quietly added and hoped for.

Forward one thing and see.

Thirty days free, no card. The first newsletter is usually the moment it makes sense.
