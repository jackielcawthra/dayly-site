The Sunday reset

Half an hour on Sunday is the cheapest week you will ever buy.

The Dayly Editors Published 19 April 2026 Updated 10 August 2026 2 min read
The short answer

Spend about thirty minutes at a fixed time each weekend looking at the week ahead, resolving the two points where it does not work, sorting kit and laundry, planning food, and telling everyone what is coming. Do it with the children present, not for them.

Weeks compound. Something small missed on Monday is a problem by Thursday; a fortnight of that and the house is running on emergencies.

The reset is a fixed half hour that stops the compounding. It is not a productivity ritual — most of it is looking at things.

The thirty minutes

Look at the week (10 min). Every commitment, every child, in one view. Find the two points where it genuinely does not work and solve those specifically. Note anything that needs buying, signing or arranging.

Kit and clothes (5 min). What is needed this week: PE, swimming, a costume, football boots. Is it clean, and is it here? This is where Thursday’s crisis gets prevented.

Food (10 min). What are we eating, on which days, and what does that require? Note the evenings that cannot contain cooking and decide those now.

Reset the house (5 min). Everyone, all together. Surfaces, floors, the things that migrated. Short and shared.

Tell everyone (2 min). Out loud, or on the calendar where they can read it. The children should know what is coming — including which evening will be difficult.

Try this

Do it at the same time every week and attach it to something that already happens — after Sunday lunch, before a particular programme. A reset that floats gets skipped, and two skipped weeks is the end of it.

Do it with the children

Not for them. Children who see the week understand why Wednesday is rushed, and they can take on parts of it: their own kit, their own deadlines, choosing a meal, noticing their own clash.

For older children, this is where they start doing their own version. A fourteen-year-old sitting with the family calendar for five minutes on a Sunday is doing something genuinely useful.

The monthly version

Once a month, add fifteen minutes:

  • Look four weeks out: trips, deadlines, birthdays, appointments, anything to book
  • Check what needs buying: shoes, uniform, kit that has stopped fitting
  • Check the areas — which jobs have quietly stopped happening
  • One thing that has been on the list for a month gets done or deleted

When you skip it

Skipping one is fine. Skipping three means the week is running you.

The recovery is not a bigger reset — it is the same thirty minutes, done next Sunday. Trying to catch up with a three-hour session is how people abandon the whole thing.

What to try this week
  1. Pick the slot and attach it to something that already happens.
  2. Do the four parts in order: week, kit, food, house.
  3. Have the children in the room for at least the first part.
  4. Add the monthly fifteen minutes at the start of next month.
