Back-to-school organisation: a four-week plan

Most of the September stress is caused by things that could have been done in August.

The Dayly Editors Published 1 August 2026 Updated 14 August 2026 3 min read Ages 4–16
The short answer

Four weeks out, sort uniform and shoes and put the term dates in. Two weeks out, restart the sleep schedule and check what still fits. One week out, do the labelling, the kit and the routines. The day before, let the children pack their own bags.

September is difficult because six weeks of no structure meets a Monday with a bell. Most of it is avoidable, and most of the work is better done in August.

Four weeks before

  • Term dates, inset days and holidays into the calendar. All of them, for the whole year.
  • Uniform and shoes. Try everything on now, not in the last week when the shops are stripped.
  • Children grow in the summer, reliably and inconveniently.
  • Anything that needs ordering — name labels, a specific bag, PE kit, a musical instrument.
  • Book the things that fill up — after-school club places, the first haircut, wraparound care.

Two weeks before

  • Start moving bedtimes. Fifteen minutes earlier every two or three days. A two-week glide is
  • much easier than a cliff on the Sunday night.
  • Restart morning wake-ups, gradually, in the last week of that fortnight.
  • Check what still fits — coats, shoes, PE kit, swimming things. The things nobody wore in July.
  • Sort the stationery with the children, which they enjoy far more than adults expect.

One week before

  • Label everything. Everything. The unlabelled jumper is not coming home.
  • Set up the launch pad — one place by the door where bags, shoes, coats and kit live. This single
  • change removes most morning searching.
  • Write the weekly kit pattern with each child: which day needs what.
  • Run the morning routine once, as a rehearsal, for younger children. Slightly silly and
  • genuinely effective.
  • Do a shop for the first week’s lunches and breakfasts, so week one is not also a food problem.
Try this

Do a full dress rehearsal one morning: get up at the school time, get dressed, have breakfast, be at the door by the leave time. Then do something enjoyable. Children take it seriously and it surfaces every gap in the plan.

The day before

  • They pack their own bag, against the list, with you nearby.
  • Uniform out, including shoes and socks and anything with a strap.
  • Early tea, early bath, early night. Expect it not to work; do it anyway.
  • Talk about it once, briefly. Long conversations about first-day nerves tend to inflate them.

The first week

Keep it light. No clubs in the first week if you can avoid it, easy dinners, early bedtimes, and low expectations about homework. Children come home from the first week of term wiped out and frequently awful, and it is not a sign of anything.

What the children should own

By age
4–7Choosing between two things. Carrying their own bag. Knowing where the launch pad is.
8–11Packing their own bag from the list. Knowing their kit days. Their own uniform out the night before.
12+The whole thing: kit, timetable, equipment, bag, and telling you what they need bought — before the last weekend of the holidays.
What to try this week
  1. Put every term date and inset day in the calendar in one sitting.
  2. Start the bedtime glide two weeks out.
  3. Build the launch pad by the door.
  4. Let each child pack their own bag the night before.
